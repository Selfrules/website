## Positioning Statement Template

```
For [TARGET AUDIENCE] who [NEED/OPPORTUNITY]
[BRAND NAME] is the [CATEGORY/FRAME OF REFERENCE]
That [KEY BENEFIT/PROMISE] because [REASON TO BELIEVE/PROOF]
```

## Current Implied Positioning Statement

Based on comprehensive content analysis of selfrules.org, the implied positioning statement is:

> **For product managers, tech leads, and founders** who struggle with cross-functional chaos (designers and developers not talking, roadmaps full but no priorities, features started but none finished), **Mattia Filippo De Luca** is the **tri-lingual PM consultant** (design + code + business) that **translates between disciplines so teams build the right thing, not just something** because he's **failed in all three roles over 13 years** and now speaks all three languages fluently.

## Positioning Strengths

1. **Exceptional problem articulation** - The "chaos" narrative is vivid and relatable
2. **Authentic failure-first credibility** - Differentiates from "guru" positioning
3. **Memorable metaphor** - "Translator" concept is unique and communicable
4. **Specific proof points** - Real numbers, real stories, real impact
5. **Clear transformation promise** - "Build the right thing, not just something"

---

## Positioning Gaps

1. **Undefined target precision** - Who exactly is this for? Industry? Company size? Role seniority?
2. **Missing category claim** - No "I am a _____" statement to anchor understanding
3. **Multiple competing services** - Consulting vs mentorship vs brainstorming - different audiences?
4. **Geographic ambiguity** - "Italy | Work anywhere" - is this global? US-focused? EU?
5. **No explicit positioning statement** - The above must be reconstructed, not read

## Recommended Positioning Statement (Refined)

> **For mid-level to senior product managers and tech leads at growth-stage companies** who are drowning in cross-functional chaos (designers and developers speak different languages, roadmaps are full but nothing ships, stakeholders all say "urgent"),
>
> **Mattia De Luca** is the **tri-lingual PM consultant** (the only one who's failed as a designer, failed as a developer, and now succeeds as a PM)
>
> That **translates between design, code, and business so your team builds the right thing Monday, not "by Q2"**
>
> Because he's **spent 13 years and €8K in painful mistakes learning to speak all three languages fluently** - and his clients consistently report going from "building features nobody uses" to "shipping the right thing in weeks."
