# Promise vs. Delivery Audit: selfrules.org

**Date:** January 27, 2026
**Task:** Subtask 5-3 - Audit promise vs delivery
**Purpose:** Map stated brand promises to supporting proof points and identify gaps

---

## Executive Summary

This audit maps the key promises made by selfrules.org's brand messaging to the evidence provided to support each promise. The analysis reveals:

- **8 core promises** identified across website content
- **Strong delivery** on 3 promises (37.5%) - fully supported with evidence
- **Moderate delivery** on 3 promises (37.5%) - partially supported, gaps exist
- **Weak delivery** on 2 promises (25%) - promise exceeds available proof

**Overall Promise-Delivery Alignment Score: 6.5/10**

The brand has exceptional proof for failure-based credibility and quantified results, but gaps exist in demonstrating speed/efficiency promises and the "identify the real problem" claim.

---

## Methodology

### Promise Identification Criteria
Promises were identified by analyzing:
1. Explicit claims in headlines, CTAs, and value propositions
2. Implicit promises through metaphors and positioning statements
3. Service-specific outcome promises
4. Brand identity claims (e.g., "translator")

### Proof Classification
| Level | Definition | Criteria |
|-------|------------|----------|
| **Strong** | Multiple diverse proof types | 3+ evidence types (testimonials, metrics, stories, credentials) |
| **Moderate** | Partial evidence exists | 1-2 evidence types, or evidence partially validates claim |
| **Weak** | Promise exceeds proof | No direct evidence, or evidence contradicts/undermines claim |

## Promise-Delivery Mapping

### PROMISE 1: Translation Between Disciplines

**Promise Statement:**
> "I translate between business, design, and code. So teams build the right thing, not just something."

**Promise Locations:**
- Footer bio (primary)
- Journey intro ("I translate. Without losing anything.")
- Superpower statement ("I speak three languages fluently")
- Services value prop ("No translators in between")

**Evidence Inventory:**

| Proof Type | Evidence | Strength |
|------------|----------|----------|
| **Career Path** | 4 roles spanning design, dev, PO, PM (13 years) | Strong |
| **Specific Languages** | Design: "visual hierarchy" / Dev: "3 sprints refactoring" / Biz: "€15K/month support tickets" | Strong |
| **Testimonials** | Marco Bianchi: "My team spoke three different languages...Mattia translated all three" | Strong |
| **Certifications** | Design implied (no cert), Dev none, PM/Scrum multiple | Moderate |
| **Portfolio Evidence** | No design portfolio, no code samples | Weak |

**Delivery Assessment: STRONG (8/10)**

**Analysis:**
The translation promise is the brand's strongest - it has layered proof from career history, explicit language examples, and a testimonial that directly validates the claim. The only gap is the lack of tangible portfolio evidence (design work, code samples) that would make the claim verifiable.

**Gap Identified:**
- Missing: Design portfolio to prove "designer language" claim
- Missing: GitHub/code samples to prove "developer language" claim

## Critical Gaps Analysis

### CRITICAL GAP 1: "The Problem Is Never What They Tell You" Lacks Proof

**Severity: HIGH** - This is positioned as a primary differentiator in the hero section.

**The Issue:**
The hero subtitle makes a bold claim that positions Mattia as someone who uncovers hidden problems. This is excellent positioning, but:
- No explicit before/after examples
- No testimonials directly validating this specific skill
- No case studies showing "stated problem → discovered problem → solution"

**Recommendation:**
1. Create 2-3 "First Meeting vs. Reality" micro-case studies
2. Request testimonials that specifically mention problem discovery
3. Add a "What I Often Find" section showing common hidden problems

**Example Format:**
```
WHAT THEY SAID: "Our developers are slow"
WHAT I FOUND: "Your specs have 47 undefined edge cases"
OUTCOME: -25% post-release incidents
```

---

### CRITICAL GAP 2: Speed Promise Lacks Timeline Evidence

**Severity: HIGH** - "Weeks, not months" and "Monday morning" are specific claims.

**The Issue:**
The brand makes speed a key selling point but provides limited evidence:
- Only one testimonial mentions a timeline (Sarah Chen: "three weeks")
- No average engagement duration stated
- "Monday morning" is used multiple times but never demonstrated

**Recommendation:**
1. Add "Typical Engagement Timeline" to services
2. Collect 3+ testimonials with specific timeframes
3. Create a "Time to First Win" metric

**Example:**
```
STRATEGIC CONSULTING: 90 minutes → Decision
BRAINSTORMING SESSION: 3 hours → Aligned Roadmap
AVERAGE TIME TO FIRST SHIPPED FEATURE: 4 weeks
```

---

### CRITICAL GAP 3: Anti-Consultant Claim Is Unverifiable

**Severity: MEDIUM** - Important for differentiation but less prominent than other promises.

**The Issue:**
"I don't sell hours. I don't sell consulting." is a category-defining claim that:
- Cannot be verified without visible pricing
- Has no comparative evidence ("Here's what a typical consultant delivers vs. me")
- Lacks testimonials mentioning the differentiated experience

**Recommendation:**
1. Add pricing transparency (even if "starting at €X" or "fixed-price engagements")
2. Create "Traditional Consulting vs. My Approach" comparison
3. Show a sample deliverable (anonymized) to demonstrate "actions not slides"

---

### MODERATE GAP 4: Translation Promise Lacks Tangible Portfolio

**Severity: MEDIUM** - Promise is well-supported but could be stronger.

**The Issue:**
The translation claim is validated by career history and testimonials, but:
- No design portfolio to prove "designer language"
- No code samples to prove "developer language"
- No visible certifications in design (despite UX claims)

**Recommendation:**
1. Add 3-5 design portfolio pieces (anonymized if needed)
2. Link to GitHub profile (if public repos exist)
3. Consider a "Design/Dev/Business" section showing work in each domain

## Key Insight: The Hero-Proof Mismatch

The most significant finding is a **mismatch between promise prominence and proof strength**:

| Promise | Prominence (Where it appears) | Proof Strength |
|---------|-------------------------------|----------------|
| Failure Wisdom | Hero headline (1st impression) | STRONG - Excellent match |
| Real Problem Discovery | Hero subtitle (2nd impression) | WEAK - Critical mismatch |
| Translation | Footer/Journey (lower prominence) | STRONG - Could be promoted |
| Anti-Consultant | Services (medium prominence) | WEAK - Moderate mismatch |

**Recommendation:** Either:
1. **Strengthen proof for hero-level promises** (add problem discovery evidence)
2. **Promote better-evidenced promises to hero** (lead with translation, supported by failure)

The current structure makes a bold claim ("the problem is never what they tell you") that the website doesn't sufficiently prove. This could undermine credibility with skeptical buyers who look for evidence.
