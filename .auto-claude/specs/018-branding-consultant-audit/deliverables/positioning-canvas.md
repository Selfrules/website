## The Complete Positioning Statement

> **For mid-to-senior product managers and product leaders at growth-stage tech companies (Series A-C, 50-500 employees)** who are drowning in cross-functional chaos—designers and developers speak different languages, roadmaps are full but nothing ships, stakeholders all say "urgent,"
>
> **Mattia De Luca** is the **Discipline Translator**—the only PM consultant who's failed as a designer, failed as a developer, and now translates between all three disciplines
>
> So your team **builds the right thing Monday, not "by Q2"**—a solution your designer understands, your developer can build, and your CFO approves
>
> Because he's **spent 13 years and €8K in painful mistakes learning to speak design, code, and business fluently**—and his clients consistently report going from "building features nobody uses" to "shipping the right thing in weeks."

## 2. Need/Opportunity

### Core Problem Statement

**The Universal Pain:**
> "Everyone says 'yes' but nobody knows what to do."

**The Specific Manifestations:**

| Problem | Evidence Quote | Frequency |
|---------|----------------|-----------|
| **Cross-functional miscommunication** | "Designer and developer not talking" | Very High |
| **False consensus** | "Everyone says 'yes' but nobody knows what to do" | Very High |
| **Priority chaos** | "Roadmap full but no priorities" | High |
| **Execution paralysis** | "4 features in development and none finished" | High |
| **Stakeholder noise** | "Feature requests from 7 different stakeholders. All urgent. All 'strategic'." | High |
| **Hidden real problems** | "The problem is never what they tell you in the first meeting" | Medium |

### The Translation Gap (Unique Insight)

**The Insight:**
> "Most PMs only speak business. Then they join a meeting and the designer says 'user journey' while the developer says 'technical debt'. Chaos."

**Why This Matters:**
Product managers are trained in business frameworks but work daily with designers and developers who speak different professional languages. The result is:

1. **Lost in translation** - Requirements change meaning as they pass between disciplines
2. **False understanding** - Everyone nods, nobody agrees
3. **Execution waste** - Features built, launched, and abandoned
4. **Talent frustration** - Good people drowning in dysfunction

### Need Articulation Assessment

| Metric | Score | Evidence |
|--------|-------|----------|
| **Clarity** | ✅ CLEAR (5/5) | Problem statements are vivid and specific |
| **Relatability** | ✅ HIGH | Visitors self-identify immediately |
| **Urgency** | ✅ HIGH | Pain is ongoing, not theoretical |

**Verdict:** This is the brand's strongest positioning element. No changes needed—protect and amplify.

### Market Opportunity Size

| Segment | Estimated Size | Addressable |
|---------|----------------|-------------|
| **PM population (global)** | ~500,000+ | Broad |
| **Mid-senior PMs at growth companies** | ~150,000 | Core ICP |
| **Experiencing cross-functional chaos** | ~75,000 (50%) | Addressable |
| **Seeking external help** | ~15,000 (20%) | Active market |

**Boutique positioning advantage:** Only need 20-50 clients/year for sustainable practice.

### Recommended Category: DISCIPLINE TRANSLATOR

**The Claim:**
> "I'm not a consultant. I'm a **Discipline Translator**—the person you call when your designer says 'user journey' while your developer says 'technical debt' and your CFO says 'just ship something.' I speak all three languages. That's not consulting. That's translation."

**Why This Category Wins:**

| Criterion | Score | Rationale |

| **Unique** | ✅ | No competitor claims this space |
| **Ownable** | ✅ | Requires actual cross-disciplinary career (competitors can't fake it) |

| **Memorable** | ✅ | Metaphor sticks; creates mental image |

### Category Origin Story

**The Narrative:**

I discovered that the real problem wasn't design or development or business—it was translation. The designer knew what users needed. The developer knew what was possible. The business knew what mattered. They just couldn't hear each other. So I became the translator."

### White Space Opportunities

#### Primary White Space: "The Practitioner's Coach"

**Position:** High Practical (7-9) × High Personal (1-3)

**Why This Space Is Empty:**

| Reason | Explanation |
|--------|-------------|
| **Economics** | 1:1 practical work is time-intensive; competitors optimize for scale |
| **Expertise Barrier** | Requires active practitioner experience, not just thought leadership |
| **Brand Positioning** | "Practical and personal" sounds less prestigious than "thought leader" |
| **Market Visibility** | Boutique providers don't have 900K audiences, so space appears empty |

**Why Mattia Wins Here:**
- Currently active as PM at QubicaAMF (not retired to advisory)
- Service model designed for 1:1/1:few engagement
- Failures are recent enough to be relatable
- Cross-functional career provides unique capability

#### Secondary White Space: Cross-Functional Translation

**Observation:** All 5 competitors are PM-to-PM communicators.

| Competitor | Communication Axis |
|------------|-------------------|

| Melissa Perri | PM Leader → PM Leader |
| **Mattia De Luca** | **Design ↔ PM ↔ Dev** |

**Opportunity:** No competitor claims to bridge design/development/business. This is ownable and defensible.
