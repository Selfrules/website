```
RICE vs ICE: which prioritization framework I actually use after 13 years as PM.

Spoiler: neither.

Here's why both frameworks fail in practice:

RICE (Reach × Impact × Confidence / Effort):
✅ Great for data-driven teams
❌ "Confidence" becomes a political number
❌ Teams game the scores to get their features prioritized

ICE (Impact × Confidence × Ease):
✅ Simpler, faster
❌ Same "Confidence" problem
❌ Ignores reach completely

What I actually use: The "Regret Minimization" Filter

For every feature request, I ask:
"If we don't ship this in 6 months, will we regret it?"

• If yes → goes to top of backlog
• If no → goes to "maybe later" pile
• If "it depends" → needs more discovery

This question cuts through all the gaming and politics.

What prioritization method actually works for your team?

—

#ProductManagement #prioritization #PM #agile
```

```
An $8K refund taught me: beautiful but confusing is decoration, not design.

2019. We shipped a redesigned checkout flow.
The design team was proud. I was proud.
Users were confused.

The result:
- 23% increase in support tickets
- 15% drop in completion rate
- One very angry enterprise client
- $8K refund

What went wrong?

We optimized for how it LOOKED.
Not for how it WORKED.

3 lessons I still use today:

1. Test with real users before launch
   (Not "the team agrees it's good")

2. Watch sessions, don't just read analytics
   (Numbers don't show frustration faces)

3. "Different" isn't "better"
   (Sometimes boring UX is brilliant UX)

The redesign after the failure?
- Kept 80% of the old flow
- Changed the 2 confusing steps
- Completion rate went UP 9%

Sometimes the best design is invisible.

What's your most expensive design lesson?

—

#ProductManagement #UX #DesignThinking #PM #failure
```
