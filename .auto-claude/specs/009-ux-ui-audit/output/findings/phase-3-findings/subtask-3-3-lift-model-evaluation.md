# Subtask 3-3: LIFT Model Evaluation

**Date:** 2026-01-27
**Auditor:** Claude (Senior UX/UI Designer Perspective)
**Framework:** WiderFunnel LIFT Model (Conversion Rate Optimization)
**URL Analyzed:** https://selfrules.org (IT/EN)

---

## Executive Summary

The LIFT Model evaluates six key factors that influence conversion rates. Applied to selfrules.org's primary conversion pages (Homepage Hero, WorkTogether section, and Google Calendar booking flow), the analysis reveals a **strong performance in Clarity (9/10) and Anxiety Reduction (8/10)**, but opportunities to improve **Urgency (5/10) and Friction (6/10)**.

**Overall LIFT Score: 7.2/10**

The site excels at communicating value and building trust but underutilizes urgency mechanics and has friction from the iframe-based booking experience.

## 1. CLARITY (9/10)

**Question:** Is the value proposition obvious in <5 seconds?

### Assessment

| Criterion | Score | Evidence |
|-----------|-------|----------|
| Headline comprehension time | 10/10 | Value prop understood in 3-5 seconds |
| Value proposition uniqueness | 9/10 | "PM who translates between design, dev, and business" is distinctive |
| Target audience identification | 9/10 | Immediately clear: teams with cross-functional dysfunction |
| Service offering clarity | 8/10 | Three services clearly defined in WorkTogether section |
| CTA clarity | 9/10 | "Parliamone" / "Let's talk" is clear, low-commitment action |

### Evidence: Homepage Hero

**Primary Headline:**
```
"I failed as a designer. Then as a developer.
Now I'm the PM you call when everyone says 'yes' but nobody knows what to do."
```

**Clarity Analysis:**
- **What** is offered: PM consulting/mediation
- **Who** it's for: Teams where cross-functional communication breaks down
- **Why** choose Mattia: 13 years spanning all three disciplines (design, dev, PM)
- **How** to engage: 15-minute call, zero pitch, zero slides

**Supporting Subtitle:**
> "Because after 13 years of expensive mistakes I learned one thing: the problem is never what they tell you in the first meeting."

This reinforces:
1. Experience depth (13 years)
2. Pragmatic approach (lessons from mistakes)
3. Unique insight (problems aren't surface-level)

### Evidence: WorkTogether Section

| Service | Clarity of Offering |
|---------|---------------------|
| **Consulting (90 min)** | "Identify the real blocker. Clear decision on what to do Monday morning." |
| **Brainstorming (2-3h)** | "Filter noise from signal. Roadmap your team understands and your bosses approve." |
| **Mentorship (monthly)** | "See your blind spots before they become 3-month problems." |

Each service includes:
- Problem statement (what pain point it solves)
- What we do (the process)
- What you get (tangible outcome)
- Feature list (specific deliverables)

### Strengths

1. **Narrative hook** - "I failed" opening is memorable and immediately establishes credibility through vulnerability
2. **Problem-first framing** - Each section leads with the pain point before presenting solutions
3. **Specificity** - Concrete numbers (13 years, 90 minutes, 15-minute call) over vague claims
4. **Colloquial language** - Avoids corporate jargon ("the meeting where everyone says yes but nothing happens")

### Improvements Needed

1. **Hero CTA specificity** - "Parliamone" / "Let's talk" could be more specific (e.g., "Book a 15-min call")
2. **Missing explicit "I help X do Y to achieve Z"** - Value prop is narrative, not formulaic
3. **No visual diagram** - Complex value prop would benefit from visual summary

### Clarity Score Justification

**9/10** - Exceptional clarity through storytelling. The only missing element is a single-sentence "I help X do Y" statement for users who skim.

## 2. RELEVANCE (8/10)

**Question:** Does the content match visitor expectations?

### Assessment

### Evidence: Content Tone Analysis

| Section | Tone | Brand Alignment |
|---------|------|-----------------|
| Hero | Vulnerable + confident | ✅ Romei pragmatism + Sinek purpose |
| Journey | Reflective + specific | ✅ Lessons from failure narrative |

### Strengths

1. **Authentic voice** - Content feels like a person, not a brand
2. **Problem recognition** - Headlines reflect actual pain points visitors experience
3. **Multiple entry points** - Different services for different needs
4. **Bilingual support** - IT/EN content is natural, not translated

1. **No audience segmentation** - Same journey for PM, founder, or tech lead
2. **Missing case studies** - Experience shown, but no detailed project walkthroughs
3. **No pricing transparency** - "15 minuti gratis" but no indication of paid rates
4. **No industry verticals** - QubicaAMF (entertainment tech) is only visible client

### Relevance Score Justification

**8/10** - Strong relevance for primary audience (PMs with cross-functional challenges). Loses points for lack of segmentation and missing case studies that enterprise buyers expect.

## 3. FRICTION (6/10 - Inverted: Lower is Better)

**Question:** What makes desired actions difficult?

### Assessment

| Friction Type | Severity | Evidence |
|---------------|----------|----------|
| Physical friction (clicks) | Medium | 3-4 clicks to booking |
| Cognitive friction | Low | Clear decision points |
| Time friction | High | 2-3s iframe load + 30-60s booking |
| Form friction | Medium | Name + email required |
| Trust friction | Low | Strong credibility signals |

### Evidence: Booking Flow Friction Points

From subtask-3-2 analysis:

| Friction Point | Impact | Description |
|----------------|--------|-------------|
| **FP-1: Iframe loading** | HIGH | 2-3s wait with only spinner (no skeleton) |
| **FP-2: Brand disconnect** | HIGH | Google Calendar UI ≠ Neobrutalist design |
| **FP-3: No pre-fill** | MEDIUM-HIGH | Must enter name/email manually |
| **FP-4: No progress indicator** | MEDIUM | No visibility into booking steps |
| **FP-5: Italian-only loading** | LOW | "Caricamento calendario..." not localized |

### Evidence: Click Path Friction

| Path | Clicks | Scroll | Assessment |
|------|--------|--------|------------|
| Hero → Book | 1 + 3 (iframe) = 4 | None | Acceptable |
| WorkTogether → Book | 1 + 3 (iframe) = 4 | ~4 viewports | Long scroll |
| AskMeAnything → Chat | 1 | ~5 viewports | Very long scroll |
| Full journey → Book | 2 + 3 = 5 | Full page | Research-then-book path |

### Evidence: Missing Friction-Reducers

| Industry Best Practice | selfrules.org Status |
|------------------------|---------------------|
| Floating CTA button | ❌ Not implemented |
| Chatbot quick-action | ❌ Chat buried in AskMeAnything |
| Pre-fetch on hover | ❌ Iframe loads on click only |
| Social login for booking | ❌ Manual form only |
| Inline booking (no modal) | ❌ Modal with iframe |
| Skeleton loading | ❌ Spinner only |

### Friction Reduction Opportunities

1. **Add floating "Book Call" button** - Visible on scroll, eliminates scroll friction
2. **Pre-fetch iframe on CTA hover** - Reduces perceived loading time
3. **Build native booking form** - Eliminate iframe brand disconnect (infrastructure exists: `bookingStore.ts`, `google-calendar.ts`)
4. **Add floating chat bubble** - Available from any scroll position
5. **Add mid-page booking prompts** - After Journey section

### Friction Score Justification

**6/10** (lower is better for friction) - The 2-3s iframe load and full-page scroll to alternative CTAs create unnecessary friction. The booking infrastructure exists to fix this but isn't used.

## 4. ANXIETY (8/10)

**Question:** What trust/reassurance elements reduce conversion fear?

### Assessment

| Anxiety Reducer | Implementation | Score |
|-----------------|----------------|-------|
| Social proof (testimonials) | 4 named testimonials | ✅ Strong |
| Experience validation | 13 years, 4 roles | ✅ Strong |
| Company names | QubicaAMF, ActiveProspect, etc. | ✅ Strong |
| Certifications | Product School, Scrum Alliance, Google | ✅ Present |
| Risk reduction | "Gratis. Senza impegno." / "Free. No commitment." | ✅ Strong |
| Process transparency | "15 minuti. Zero pitch. Zero slides." | ✅ Strong |
| Outcome specificity | "What to do Monday morning" | ✅ Strong |
| Vulnerability | "I failed as a designer" | ✅ Differentiating |

### Evidence: Trust Architecture

**Layer 1: Immediate Trust (Hero)**
- "13 years of expensive mistakes" - Experience through humility
- "UX • CODE • PM" badge - Multi-disciplinary credibility
- Headline acknowledges failures - Counter-intuitive trust builder

**Layer 2: Validated Trust (Journey)**
- Named companies: QubicaAMF, ActiveProspect, FLOWING, Selfrules
- Quantified achievements:
  - "+30 top-ups in one Sunday"
  - "-12% payment processing time"
  - "-25% post-release incidents"
  - "+9% integration adoption"
- Certifications with issuers: Product School, Scrum Alliance, Google

**Layer 3: Social Proof (WorkTogether)**
- 4 testimonials with names, roles, and companies
- Specific outcomes mentioned ("Three weeks later, we shipped the right thing")
- Variety of roles: Founder/CEO, PM, Tech Lead, Senior Designer

**Layer 4: Risk Mitigation (CTA)**
```
"Una call da 15 minuti"
"Gratis. Senza impegno."
"Zero pitch. Zero slides. Just your problem and how to unblock it."
```

### Evidence: Anxiety-Inducing Gaps

| Missing Element | Anxiety Impact | Recommendation |
|-----------------|----------------|----------------|
| **No headshot/photo** | Medium - missing human connection | Add professional photo to Hero |
| **No client logos** | Medium - missing brand association | Add logo bar below Hero |
| **No pricing indication** | High - uncertainty about cost | Add "Starting at X" or "Typical engagement" |
| **No case studies** | Medium - missing depth for enterprise | Add 1-2 detailed case studies |
| **No privacy assurance on form** | Low - standard industry practice | Add micro-copy on anonymous form |

### Anxiety Reducer Analysis

| Reducer | Effectiveness | Evidence |
|---------|---------------|----------|
| **Vulnerability as credibility** | Very High | "I failed" opening is counterintuitive and memorable |
| **Free, no-commitment offer** | Very High | Removes financial/time risk |
| **Specific outcomes** | High | "What to do Monday morning" is concrete |
| **Named testimonials** | High | Real people, real companies, real results |
| **Quantified achievements** | High | Numbers > adjectives |
| **Certifications** | Medium | Establishes baseline competence |

### Anxiety Score Justification

**8/10** - Excellent trust architecture with vulnerability-first approach. Missing headshot and pricing transparency are the main gaps.

## 5. URGENCY (5/10)

**Question:** Is there motivation to act now rather than later?

### Assessment

| Urgency Type | Implementation | Score |
|--------------|----------------|-------|
| Scarcity (limited availability) | ❌ Not present | 0/10 |
| Time-based offers | ❌ Not present | 0/10 |
| Implicit urgency (problem pain) | ✅ Present | 7/10 |
| FOMO / social proof | ⚠️ Weak | 4/10 |
| External deadline | ❌ Not present | 0/10 |

### Evidence: Urgency Indicators Present

**Implicit Urgency (Pain-Driven):**
- "When everyone says 'yes' but nobody knows what to do" - Ongoing frustration
- "4 features in development and none finished?" - Immediate recognition
- "Designer and developer not talking?" - Current problem acknowledgment
- "Monday, not 'by Q2'" - Contrasts with slow corporate timelines

**Outcome Urgency (Result Speed):**
- "Clear decision on what to do Monday morning"
- "Three weeks later, we shipped the right thing" (testimonial)
- "Market calmed in one month with only 3 interventions"

### Evidence: Urgency Indicators Missing

| Industry Urgency Tactic | selfrules.org Status |
|-------------------------|---------------------|
| "Only X slots available this week" | ❌ Not shown |
| "Booking filling up" | ❌ Not shown |
| "Limited spots for mentorship" | ❌ Not shown |
| "Price increases on [date]" | ❌ Not shown |
| "Currently accepting Y clients" | ❌ Not shown |
| Live booking counter | ❌ Not shown |
| "X people booked this week" | ❌ Not shown |

### Urgency Gap Analysis

**Why urgency is minimal:**
1. **Positioning as premium** - High-touch consulting doesn't use scarcity tactics
2. **Trust-first approach** - Pressure tactics may conflict with authenticity
3. **Evergreen content** - No time-sensitive elements

**Strategic Urgency Opportunities (Brand-Aligned):**

| Tactic | Brand Fit | Implementation |
|--------|-----------|----------------|
| **"Currently accepting 3 new clients"** | ✅ Authentic scarcity | Add to WorkTogether CTA banner |
| **"Next available slot: [date]"** | ✅ Factual, not pushy | Fetch from Google Calendar API |
| **Testimonial recency** | ✅ Social proof urgency | "Sarah booked 2 weeks ago and shipped in 3" |
| **Problem escalation** | ✅ Pain-based urgency | "Every week of misalignment costs €X" |
| **Countdown timer** | ❌ Too salesy | Conflicts with brand |

### Urgency Score Justification

**5/10** - Implicit urgency through problem recognition is present, but no explicit urgency mechanisms. The site assumes visitors will return, which may lose impulse converters.

## Recommendations by LIFT Factor

### Clarity Quick Wins
1. Add micro-copy below Hero CTA: "15-minute call, zero commitment"
2. Add "I help X do Y to achieve Z" summary sentence

### Relevance Quick Wins
1. Add 1-2 case studies with problem → process → outcome structure
2. Indicate pricing range ("Typical engagements start at €X")

### Urgency Quick Wins
1. Display next available slot from Google Calendar ("Next slot: Tuesday 2pm")
2. Add "Currently accepting 3 new clients" to WorkTogether banner
3. Add testimonial timestamps ("Sarah booked 2 weeks ago")

### Friction Quick Wins
1. Add floating "Book Call" button (visible on scroll)
2. Pre-fetch iframe on CTA hover (reduce perceived load time)
3. Add skeleton UI for calendar loading state
4. Consider Cal.com or custom booking for brand consistency

### Anxiety Quick Wins
1. Add headshot/professional photo to Hero
2. Add client logo bar below Hero
3. Add micro-copy on anonymous form: "100% anonymous. No email required."

### Distraction Quick Wins
1. Reduce floating shape opacity on Hero or remove one
2. Remove placeholder footer links (Resources section)
3. Add floating chat bubble for instant access
