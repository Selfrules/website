Perform a detailed UX analysis of each competitor identified in subtask 4-1, evaluating navigation, CTAs, trust signals, booking/contact flow, and mobile experience. Each competitor is scored against selfrules.org to identify gaps and opportunities.

## Scoring Methodology

Each UX dimension is scored 1-10:
- **1-3**: Poor / Missing
- **4-5**: Below average / Incomplete
- **6-7**: Adequate / Industry standard
- **8-9**: Strong / Best-in-class
- **10**: Exceptional / Innovative

## Competitor UX Patterns Worth Adopting

### 1. Quantified Social Proof (from Gibson Biddle)
**Pattern**: Display NPS scores, subscriber counts, and measurable outcomes prominently.
**Why**: Numbers are processed faster than testimonial text. Gibson's "NPS=69" communicates quality instantly.
**Application to selfrules.org**: Add metrics like "X teams helped", "Y% improvement delivered", "Z years experience" as visual callouts near the hero or WorkTogether section.

### 2. Graduated Engagement Ladder (from Teresa Torres)
**Pattern**: Offer free content > low-cost resource > premium course > enterprise training as ascending commitment levels.
**Why**: Reduces the psychological leap from "stranger" to "client". Each step builds trust.
**Application to selfrules.org**: Add newsletter signup (free) > downloadable framework/template (email capture) > 15-min call (current CTA) > consulting engagement. This creates 4 conversion points instead of 1.

### 3. Intent-Based Navigation (from Teresa Torres)
**Pattern**: Nav labels that match visitor intent ("Our Courses", "Train Your Team", "Articles") rather than site structure.
**Why**: Visitors think in terms of their goals, not your sitemap.
**Application to selfrules.org**: Rename/add nav items like "Work with me", "My approach", "Read my blog", "Book a call" - each matching a distinct visitor intent.

### 4. Premium Minimalism as Trust Signal (from Lenny Rachitsky)
**Pattern**: High design quality with generous whitespace communicates success and professionalism.
**Why**: Visual quality correlates with perceived competence. The "halo effect" of good design transfers to perceived service quality.
**Application to selfrules.org**: The neobrutalist system already achieves this through intentional boldness. Ensure execution polish matches the ambition - any rough edges (broken animations, inconsistent spacing) will undermine the effect.

### 5. Calendar Availability Preview (from Gibson Biddle)
**Pattern**: Show upcoming availability dates directly on the site before requiring booking action.
**Why**: Creates urgency ("only 3 slots left in February") and transparency. Reduces the friction of "I wonder if they're available."
**Application to selfrules.org**: Display a "Next available slots" preview near the booking CTA, showing 2-3 upcoming openings. This adds urgency without requiring the full Google Calendar flow.

## Patterns to Avoid

### 1. Email-Only Contact (Gibson Biddle)
Gibson's reliance on a Gmail address for all inquiries is a significant friction point. selfrules.org's automated calendar booking is already superior.

### 2. Zero Contact Path (Lenny Rachitsky)
Lenny's complete absence of a contact mechanism works only at extreme scale. selfrules.org correctly prioritizes direct engagement.

### 3. Navigation Overload (Teresa Torres)
With 7 nav items, Teresa approaches the cognitive limit. selfrules.org should keep navigation under 5-6 items.

### 4. Text-Only CTAs (Gibson Biddle)
Gibson's plain-text links as CTAs have nearly zero visual prominence. selfrules.org's neobrutalist buttons are dramatically more effective.
