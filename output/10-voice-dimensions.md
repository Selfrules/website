# Voice Dimensions Analysis: selfrules.org vs. Benchmarks

**Audit Phase:** 3 - Competitive Benchmarking
**Subtask:** 3.3 - Rate Voice Dimensions (1-10): Formality, Energy, Humor, Authority
**Date:** 2026-01-26
**Analyst:** Art Director Audit (Claude)

---

## Executive Summary

This document rates selfrules.org and 5 benchmark personal brands across four key voice dimensions, revealing how brand voice contributes to positioning strength and audience connection.

**Key Finding:** selfrules.org has a distinctive voice that balances approachability with credibility, but it occupies a "middle zone" on most dimensions—neither fully casual nor fully authoritative. This creates a coherent but potentially unmemorable voice profile. The most successful benchmarks occupy **extreme positions** on at least one dimension.

---

## Voice Dimensions Framework

Each brand is rated on a 1-10 scale across four dimensions:

| Dimension | 1 (Low) | 10 (High) | What It Measures |
|-----------|---------|-----------|------------------|
| **Formality** | Casual, conversational | Formal, professional | Language register and tone |
| **Energy** | Calm, measured | Enthusiastic, dynamic | Emotional intensity and pace |
| **Humor** | Serious, direct | Playful, witty | Use of levity and personality |
| **Authority** | Humble, peer-level | Authoritative, expert | Confidence level in claims |

## Part 4: Strategic Voice Recommendations

### Voice Dimension Adjustments for selfrules.org

Based on competitive benchmarking and positioning analysis:

#### Option A: Increase Authority (Recommended)

**Current:** Authority 6/10
**Target:** Authority 7-8/10

**Rationale:** The "translator" positioning requires credibility. Competitors with similar positioning (Dan Mall, Sara Soueidan) score 8/10 on authority.

**Implementation:**
- Add social proof (testimonials, client names, metrics)
- More direct expertise claims ("In 13 years, I've translated 200+ products...")
- Display credentials or recognition
- Professional headshot to anchor authority

#### Option B: Increase Humor/Personality

**Current:** Humor 5/10
**Target:** Humor 7/10

**Rationale:** The failure narrative and irony could lean more into personality, similar to Josh Comeau's friendly energy.

**Implementation:**
- More self-deprecating stories
- Visual humor elements (like Lynn Fisher's weird projects)
- Interactive elements that show personality

#### Option C: Double Down on Low Formality

**Current:** Formality 3/10
**Target:** Formality 2/10 (push to extreme)

**Rationale:** If "translator" means speaking everyone's language, be the most approachable person in the room.

**Implementation:**
- Even more conversational copy
- Visual style that feels "anti-design"
- Explicit "no jargon" promise

## Part 6: Key Insights & Conclusions

### Voice Dimension Summary

1. **selfrules.org has an authentic, coherent voice** — The conversational tone, problem-first approach, and self-deprecating humor create genuine personality.

2. **The voice lacks a memorable extreme** — Successful competitors occupy at least one extreme position. selfrules.org's all-mid-range profile is pleasant but forgettable.

3. **Authority is the critical gap** — The "translator PM" positioning claims expertise, but the voice signals peer-level (6/10) rather than expert-level (8/10) authority.

4. **Voice-visual mismatch persists** — Verbal voice says "approachable translator"; visual voice says "design-insider gatekeeper."

### Actionable Recommendations

| Priority | Recommendation | Impact |
|----------|----------------|--------|
| **High** | Add authority signals (testimonials, metrics, credentials) | Moves Authority from 6→8 |
| **High** | Maintain conversational formality (don't go corporate) | Preserves Formality at 3 |
| **Medium** | Increase energy slightly through more direct claims | Moves Energy from 6→7 |
| **Medium** | Lean into personality/humor where natural | Moves Humor from 5→6 |
| **Low** | Consider extreme position in ONE dimension | Creates memorability anchor |

### Final Voice Positioning Statement

**Current Voice:** "Pragmatic peer who speaks your language"
**Target Voice:** "Approachable expert who's been in your shoes"

The shift from "peer" to "expert" requires:
- Social proof (metrics, testimonials)
- Explicit expertise claims
- Visual authority (professional photography)
- Maintained conversational warmth
