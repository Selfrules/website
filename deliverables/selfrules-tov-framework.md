**Created:** 2026-01-27
**Author:** Copywriter-Hybrid Agent (Pragmatic Provocateur Voice)
**Version:** 1.0
**Purpose:** Actionable reference for maintaining consistent voice across all selfrules.org content, in both Italian and English
**Applies to:** Website copy, blog posts, CTAs, case studies, social media, email sequences, project descriptions, bio/about sections

## 1. VOICE PHILOSOPHY: THE PRAGMATIC PROVOCATEUR

selfrules.org speaks with one unified voice built from three communication philosophies. **This is not three voices bolted together — it's one person who thinks clearly, speaks humanly, and connects purposefully.**

### The Three Pillars

| Pillar | Source | Core Principle | Default Weight | Role in Copy |
|--------|--------|---------------|----------------|--------------|
| **Clarity Through Subtraction** | Jacopo Romei | Every word must earn its place. Cut ruthlessly. | 50% | Structure, directness, moral clarity |
| **Accessibility Through Honesty** | Kate Toon | Make the complex feel conquerable. Admit vulnerability. | 30% | Relatability, humor, permission-giving |
| **Purpose Through Connection** | Simon Sinek | Always connect action to "why." | 20% | Meaning, curiosity, transformation framing |

### How They Work Together

Think of it as a cocktail, not a checklist:

- **Romei** provides the bones — the structure, the punch, the "this is wrong" moments
- **Toon** provides the skin — the warmth, the "that's me!" recognition, the self-deprecation
- **Sinek** provides the heartbeat — the "why this matters" undercurrent

**The test:** Would Romei call it clear? Would Toon find it relatable? Would Sinek see the purpose? If yes to all three: publish.

## 2. CONTENT TYPE CALIBRATION TABLE

Voice proportions shift based on what you're writing. **Never apply the same ratio everywhere.**

| Content Type | Romei % | Toon % | Sinek % | Primary Tone | Max Length | Key Objective |
|-------------|---------|--------|---------|-------------|-----------|--------------|
| **CTAs** | 70 | 20 | 10 | Ultra-direct | 2-5 words | Trigger immediate action |
| **Hero Sections** | 40 | 30 | 30 | Challenge + hook | 6-10 words headline | Create curiosity gap |
| **Blog Posts** | 50 | 30 | 20 | Story-driven argument | 500-2000 words | Deliver insight through narrative |
| **Case Studies** | 35 | 20 | 45 | Purpose-driven narrative | 500-1000 words | Show transformation, not features |
| **Project Descriptions** | 60 | 25 | 15 | Clear, outcome-focused | 100-200 words | Explain what happened + result |
| **About/Bio** | 30 | 50 | 20 | Authentic, credible | 50-150 words | Subvert traditional format |
| **Email Sequences** | 35 | 45 | 20 | Conversational, valuable | 200-400 words | Build relationship through value |
| **Social Media** | 30 | 50 | 20 | Human, specific | 50-100 words | Start conversations |
| **Service Descriptions** | 60 | 25 | 15 | Pain → outcome | 50-100 words per tier | Make the offer undeniable |
| **Testimonials (curated)** | 40 | 30 | 30 | Transformation story | 40-80 words | Prove claims through others' words |
| **Microcopy (buttons, labels, tooltips)** | 70 | 25 | 5 | Functional clarity | 1-8 words | Remove friction |
| **Footer/Navigation** | 50 | 40 | 10 | Warm + functional | 2-10 words per item | Guide without corporate tone |

### How to Read This Table

When writing a **CTA** (70/20/10):
- 70% of your effort goes to Romei: make it impossibly clear, use action verbs, cut every unnecessary word
- 20% Toon: make it feel human, not robotic ("Parliamone" not "Submit request")
- 10% Sinek: hint at the outcome ("Start the conversation" implies purpose)

When writing a **Case Study** (35/20/45):
- 45% of your effort goes to Sinek: lead with "why this matters," frame as transformation
- 35% Romei: keep the narrative tight, use specific details and numbers
- 20% Toon: include human moments, make the client relatable

## 3. QUALITY CHECKLISTS

Run these checks **before publishing any content**. Check the relevant items based on the content type's dominant voice.

### 3A. ROMEI CHECK (Clarity Through Subtraction)

Use for ALL content types. Weight more heavily for CTAs, Service Descriptions, Project Descriptions.

| # | Check | How to Verify |
|---|-------|---------------|
| R1 | **Could you cut 20% more words without losing meaning?** | Re-read aloud. Every "that," "very," "really," "just" is suspect. |
| R2 | **Have you made a clear, definitive statement?** | Look for hedging: "maybe," "perhaps," "it could be." Replace with stances. |
| R3 | **Is there at least one "this is wrong" moment?** | Point to one conventional wisdom you're challenging. If nothing challenged, add one. |
| R4 | **Have you bolded 1-3 key insights per section?** | Scan visually. Bold = takeaway. Never bold full sentences — only phrases. |
| R5 | **Did you tell a specific story before introducing theory?** | Check paragraph 1. If it starts with a principle or definition, reorder. |
| R6 | **Are paragraphs 1-3 sentences?** | Count. If any paragraph exceeds 4 sentences, split it. |
| R7 | **Zero corporate jargon?** | Ctrl+F: "leverage," "synergy," "best-in-class," "solution" (when vague), "optimize," "robust." Kill them all. |

### 3B. TOON CHECK (Accessibility Through Honesty)

Use for ALL content types. Weight more heavily for About/Bio, Email, Social Media.

| # | Check | How to Verify |
|---|-------|---------------|
| T1 | **Would someone read this and think "that's me!"?** | Read as your target reader. If nothing triggers recognition, add a relatable detail. |
| T2 | **Have you included at least one relatable, human detail?** | Look for specifics: times, places, feelings, everyday situations. |
| T3 | **Have you subverted or challenged traditional business advice?** | If the copy could appear on any generic consulting site, it fails this check. |
| T4 | **Are you giving permission, not prescribing?** | Replace "you should" with "it's okay to" or "we can." |
| T5 | **Is there constructive irony or humor?** | Not forced. One moment where you point out an absurdity. |
| T6 | **Does it use "we/us" more than "you should"?** | Count pronouns. "We" = inclusive. "You should" = prescriptive. |
| T7 | **Would you say this to a friend over coffee?** | If it sounds like a press release, rewrite it. |

### 3C. SINEK CHECK (Purpose Through Connection)

Use for ALL content types. Weight more heavily for Case Studies, Hero Sections, Blog Posts.

| # | Check | How to Verify |
|---|-------|---------------|
| S1 | **Have you connected the action to a "why"?** | Every CTA, recommendation, or claim needs a "because." |
| S2 | **Did you use a question-based opening or create curiosity?** | Check first line. Questions or provocative statements beat definitions. |
| S3 | **Is there a clear before/after or contrasting story?** | Look for transformation: "Before X, now Y." If absent, add one. |
| S4 | **Does it end with hope/possibility, not just instruction?** | Last paragraph should leave the reader with something to aspire to, not a to-do list. |
| S5 | **Is the reader the hero, not the author?** | Count "I/me" vs "you/your." The reader should be center stage. |

### Quick Reference: Which Checks to Prioritize by Content Type

| Content Type | Must-Pass Romei | Must-Pass Toon | Must-Pass Sinek |
|-------------|-----------------|----------------|-----------------|
| CTAs | R1, R2, R7 | T7 | S1 |
| Hero Sections | R2, R3, R6 | T1, T2 | S2, S3 |
| Blog Posts | R4, R5, R6 | T1, T3, T5 | S1, S3, S4 |
| Case Studies | R1, R5 | T2 | S1, S3, S4 |
| Service Descriptions | R1, R2, R7 | T1, T4 | S1 |
| About/Bio | R6 | T1, T2, T3, T7 | S5 |
| Email Sequences | R1, R6 | T1, T4, T6, T7 | S1, S4 |
| Social Media | R1 | T1, T5, T7 | S2 |

## 4. DO / DON'T EXAMPLES

All examples are drawn from selfrules.org actual content and audit findings. **These are not generic — they reflect the specific brand voice.**

### 4A. Headlines & Hero Copy

| ✅ DO | ❌ DON'T | Why |
|-------|---------|-----|
| "Ho fallito come designer. Poi come developer. Ora sono il PM che chiami quando tutti dicono 'sì' ma nessuno sa cosa fare." | "Experienced Product Manager with 13+ years of cross-functional leadership in design, development, and business strategy." | DO leads with vulnerability + specific scenario. DON'T is a LinkedIn bio. |
| "Perché faccio l'interprete nei meeting (senza parlare lingue straniere)" | "Why Cross-Functional Communication Matters in Product Management" | DO uses metaphor + humor (parenthetical). DON'T is a conference title. |
| "Most PMs only speak business. Chaos." | "Effective product management requires bridging communication gaps between teams." | DO is punchy, visual, one-word punch. DON'T is a textbook. |

### 4B. Value Propositions

| ✅ DO | ❌ DON'T | Why |
|-------|---------|-----|
| "Non vendo ore. Non vendo consulenze. Vendo una soluzione che il tuo designer capisce, il tuo developer può buildare, e il tuo CFO approva." | "I offer comprehensive consulting services tailored to your team's needs." | DO names specific stakeholders + outcomes. DON'T uses "comprehensive" and "tailored" (corporate zombies). |
| "Senza traduttori in mezzo." | "Streamlined communication for optimal efficiency." | DO is vivid, callback to core metaphor. DON'T is jargon soup. |
| "Decisione chiara su cosa fare lunedì mattina. Non slide. Azioni." | "Deliverables include a detailed action plan and strategic roadmap." | DO gives a time anchor + contrasts (not slides). DON'T lists deliverables like a proposal. |

### 4C. CTAs

| ✅ DO | ❌ DON'T | Why |
|-------|---------|-----|
| "Parliamone" (Let's talk about it) | "Submit inquiry" | DO is conversational, inclusive. DON'T is a form label. |
| "Prenotiamo 15 minuti" (Let's book 15 minutes) | "Schedule a consultation" | DO uses inclusive "let's" + specific time. DON'T sounds like a medical appointment. |
| "Ancora qui? Vai su e prenota quella call che rimandi da stamattina" | "Don't miss out — book your session today!" | DO calls out procrastination directly (playful). DON'T is generic urgency. |
| "Lunedì, non 'entro Q2'." | "Get started today!" | DO creates accountability with specific contrast. DON'T is empty urgency. |

### 4D. Testimonials (Curated / Guided)

| ✅ DO | ❌ DON'T | Why |
|-------|---------|-----|
| "Abbiamo passato 6 mesi a costruire funzionalità che nessuno usava. Una sessione di 90 minuti con Mattia..." | "Mattia is a highly skilled professional who delivered excellent results." | DO tells a transformation story with specific timeline (6 months → 90 min → 3 weeks). DON'T is a generic endorsement. |
| "Il mio team parlava tre lingue diverse: design voleva bellezza, engineering voleva codice pulito, business voleva fatturato." | "Mattia helped improve our team communication significantly." | DO uses the brand's "three languages" metaphor with specifics. DON'T uses "significantly" (the corporate "I have nothing specific to say"). |

### 4E. Service Descriptions

| ✅ DO | ❌ DON'T | Why |
|-------|---------|-----|
| "Feature request da 7 stakeholder diversi. Tutti urgenti. Tutti 'strategici'." | "Manage competing priorities from multiple stakeholders." | DO uses specific number (7), ironic quotes on 'strategic', and sentence fragments for punch. DON'T is a job posting. |
| "Roadmap che il tuo team capisce e i tuoi capi approvano." | "Develop a stakeholder-aligned product roadmap." | DO names two specific outcomes (team + bosses). DON'T uses "stakeholder-aligned" (jargon). |

### 4F. Body Copy / Blog Posts

| ✅ DO | ❌ DON'T | Why |
|-------|---------|-----|
| "La maggior parte dei PM parla solo business. Poi arriva in meeting e il designer dice user journey mentre lo sviluppatore dice technical debt. Caos. Io traduco." | "Cross-functional alignment is critical for product success. Teams often struggle with different vocabularies across disciplines." | DO paints a scene with specific dialogue. DON'T explains abstractly. |
| Start with: "I had three architectural solutions and no idea which to choose." | Start with: "In today's complex technological landscape..." | DO opens with relatable human moment. DON'T opens with a thought leadership cliché. |

## 5. SIGNATURE PHRASES & LINGUISTIC PATTERNS

These are the verbal fingerprints of the selfrules.org voice. Use them naturally — never force them.

### 5A. Signature Phrases

| Phrase (IT) | Phrase (EN) | When to Use | Voice Source |
|------------|------------|-------------|-------------|
| "Questo è sbagliato." | "This is wrong." | Opening a challenge to conventional wisdom | Romei |
| "Ti faccio un esempio." | "Let me give you an example." | Transitioning from principle to story | Romei |
| "Ecco cosa nessuno ti dice su [topic]:" | "Here's what nobody tells you about [topic]:" | Insider knowledge hook | Romei |
| "Se sei come me..." | "If you're anything like me..." | Creating relatability | Toon |
| "**Non si tratta di [tattica] — si tratta di [significato].**" | "**This isn't about [tactic] — it's about [meaning].**" | Reframing from feature to purpose | Sinek |
| "La maggior parte pensa X. È il contrario." | "Most people think X. This is backwards." | Contrarian setup | Romei + Sinek |
| "Come si è scoperto poi..." | "As it turns out..." | Surprising insight transition | Toon |
| "Non vendo [cosa commodity]. Vendo [cosa di valore]." | "I don't sell [commodity]. I sell [value]." | Reframing the offer | Romei |
| "Lunedì, non 'entro Q2'." | "Monday, not 'by Q2'." | Creating time urgency with contrast | Romei |
| "La cosa giusta, non solo qualcosa." | "The right thing, not just something." | Quality over output framing | Sinek |

### 5B. Linguistic Patterns to Replicate

| Pattern | Example | Effect |
|---------|---------|--------|
| **Parallel negation → affirmation** | "Non vendo ore. Non vendo consulenze. Vendo una soluzione..." | Builds tension through negation, releases with clarity |
| **One-word punch** | "Caos." / "Azioni." / "Chaos." | Stops the reader. Maximum impact. |
| **Parenthetical humor** | "(senza parlare lingue straniere)" | Adds personality without derailing the main point |
| **Rhetorical self-question** | "Perché? Perché dopo 13 anni..." | Sinek-style curiosity creation |
| **Ironic air quotes** | "Tutti 'strategici'." | Acknowledges corporate BS the reader recognizes |
| **Time contrast** | "Lunedì, non 'entro Q2'" / "6 mesi → 90 minuti → 3 settimane" | Creates urgency through concrete time comparison |
| **Scene painting** | "il designer dice user journey mentre lo sviluppatore dice technical debt" | Creates a mental movie the reader recognizes |
| **Inclusive CTA language** | "Parliamone" / "Prenotiamo" / "Lavoriamo insieme" | Italian "noi" form creates collaboration, not command |

### 5C. Words to Always Use vs. Words to Never Use

| ✅ ALWAYS | ❌ NEVER | Replacement |
|-----------|---------|-------------|
| "il problema è..." (the problem is) | "la sfida è..." (the challenge is) | "Challenge" softens what needs sharpness |
| "costruire" (build) | "implementare" (implement) | "Build" is tangible; "implement" is corporate |
| "team" | "risorse" (resources) | People aren't resources |
| "funziona" (works) | "ottimizzato" (optimized) | Either it works or it doesn't |
| "errore" (mistake) | "area di miglioramento" (area for improvement) | Own the failure, don't euphemize |
| "soldi" / "costi" (money / costs) | "investimento" (investment) | Unless it genuinely IS an investment |
| "chiaro" (clear) | "best-in-class" | English jargon creeping into Italian copy |
| "perché" (why/because) | "al fine di" (in order to) | Bureaucratic language kills connection |

## 6. READABILITY TARGETS

Every piece of content must hit these targets. Measure before publishing.

### 6A. Core Metrics

| Metric | Target Range | How to Measure | Why This Range |
|--------|-------------|----------------|----------------|
| **Flesch Reading Ease** | 60-70 | Hemingway App, readable.io, MS Word | Conversational but not dumbed down. Sweet spot for educated professionals who want clarity. |
| **Grade Level** | 6th-8th grade | Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level | Forces simplicity. Technical concepts explained in everyday language. |
| **Average Sentence Length** | 14-20 words | Manual count or tool | Allows variety (5-word punches + 25-word explanations) while keeping average readable. |
| **Average Paragraph Length** | 2-4 sentences | Manual count | Forces white space. Prevents wall-of-text syndrome. |
| **Bold Density** | 1-3 key phrases per section | Visual scan | Enables skimming without losing core message. |

### 6B. Content-Specific Readability Targets

| Content Type | Flesch Target | Avg Sentence | Avg Paragraph | Special Rules |
|-------------|---------------|--------------|---------------|---------------|
| CTAs | 80+ | 3-8 words | N/A (single element) | Monosyllabic preferred |
| Hero Sections | 65-75 | 8-15 words | 1-2 sentences | Short, punchy, rhythm-driven |
| Blog Posts | 60-70 | 14-20 words | 2-4 sentences | Vary sentence length for rhythm |
| Case Studies | 55-65 | 15-22 words | 3-4 sentences | Slightly denser — technical audience |
| Service Descriptions | 65-75 | 10-16 words | 1-3 sentences | Ultra-scannable |
| Email Sequences | 65-75 | 12-18 words | 1-3 sentences | Conversational — read aloud test |
| Social Media | 70-80 | 8-14 words | 1-2 sentences | Stop-scroll brevity |

### 6C. Readability Red Flags

If you notice any of these, rewrite immediately:

- ❌ Paragraph over 5 sentences
- ❌ Sentence over 30 words
- ❌ Three consecutive sentences starting with the same word
- ❌ Passive voice in CTAs or headlines
- ❌ More than 2 bulleted lists per piece (max 3-5 items each)
- ❌ Flesch Reading Ease below 50 (too academic)
- ❌ No bold text in a section with 3+ paragraphs

## 7. ACTIONABLE TEMPLATES

Use these templates as starting structures. **Adapt, don't copy-paste.**

### 7A. CTA Template (70% Romei / 20% Toon / 10% Sinek)

```
[Button text]: [Action verb] + [specific outcome/time]
[Supporting text]: [Pain question]? [Low-friction promise]. [Time anchor].

Example (IT):
Button: "Prenotiamo 15 minuti"
Supporting: "Designer e developer che non si parlano? Zero pitch. Solo il tuo problema. Lunedì."

Example (EN):
Button: "Let's book 15 minutes"
Supporting: "Designer and developer not talking? Zero pitch. Just your problem. Monday."
```

**Rules:**
- Button: 2-5 words, always include time or outcome
- Supporting: Open with pain question, follow with objection removal, close with time anchor
- Use inclusive language ("Let's" not "Book")

### 7B. Hero Section Template (40% Romei / 30% Sinek / 30% Toon)

```
Headline: [Vulnerability/failure hook] + [current expertise/role]
Subheadline: [Curiosity question]? [Insider insight from experience].
CTA 1: [Conversation starter] — e.g., "Parliamone"
CTA 2: [Story continuation] — e.g., "Come sono arrivato qui"

Example (IT):
Headline: "Ho fallito come [ruolo 1]. Poi come [ruolo 2]. Ora [risultato/posizionamento]."
Subheadline: "Perché? Perché dopo [N] anni di [errori specifici] ho capito una cosa: [insight controintuitivo]."

Example (EN):
Headline: "I failed as a [role 1]. Then as a [role 2]. Now [result/positioning]."
Subheadline: "Why? Because after [N] years of [specific mistakes] I learned one thing: [counterintuitive insight]."
```

**Rules:**
- Headline max 20 words
- Must include personal story or challenge
- Subheadline answers "why should I care?"
- Two CTAs: one action, one exploration

### 7C. Blog Post Template (50% Romei / 30% Toon / 20% Sinek)

```
## [Provocative Title in Sentence Case]

### Opening Hook (2-3 paragraphs)
[Relatable scenario OR provocative challenge]
"If you're anything like me..." / "Most people think X. This is backwards."

### The Story (3-5 paragraphs)
[Concrete narrative with visual details and stakes]
Specific names, numbers, timelines. Paint the scene.

### The Analysis (2-4 paragraphs)
[Extract principles. **Bold the 1-3 key insights.**]
"Here's what nobody tells you about [topic]:"

### The Practical Application (2-3 paragraphs)
[Make it actionable. What can the reader do Monday morning?]
Maximum 1 bulleted list, 3-5 items.

### Conclusion (1-2 paragraphs)
[Return to opening tension. State implications with hope.]
"This isn't about [tactic] — it's about [meaning]."
```

**Rules:**
- 500-2000 words total
- Story before theory, always
- One "this is wrong" moment minimum
- End with hope, not homework

### 7D. Case Study Template (35% Romei / 20% Toon / 45% Sinek)

```
## [Client Name]: [Transformation in 5-8 Words]

### The Challenge
[Start with the gap. Frame as human problem, not technical.]
"[Client] had been [relatable struggle] for [time period]."

### The Real Problem
[Challenge the surface explanation. What was ACTUALLY broken?]
"But the real issue wasn't [obvious thing] — it was [deeper thing]."

### The Approach
[Story-driven. What actually happened in the room?]
Specific questions asked, decisions made, "aha" moments.

### The Transformation
[Before/after contrast. Focus on impact, not output.]
"Before: [pain]. After: [relief + measurable result]."
Include timeline: "In [X weeks/months], [specific result]."

### The Lesson
[Universal principle anyone can apply.]
"**[Insight that transcends this specific case.]**"
```

**Rules:**
- Lead with why, not what
- Include at least 2 specific metrics (time saved, revenue impact, etc.)
- The client is the hero, not Mattia
- End with a transferable lesson

### 7E. Service Description Template (60% Romei / 25% Toon / 15% Sinek)

```
### [Service Name] ([Duration/Format])

**The problem:**
"[Specific pain scenario]? [Second pain scenario]?"
Use questions. Use numbers. Use ironic air quotes if appropriate.

**What you get:**
"[Specific deliverable + time anchor]. Non [what it's NOT]. [What it IS]."

**Example:**
Problem: "Roadmap piena ma nessuna priorità? Designer e developer non si parlano?"
Deliverable: "Decisione chiara su cosa fare lunedì mattina. Non slide. Azioni."
```

**Rules:**
- Problem always first (never features)
- Deliverable uses contrast ("Not X. Y.")
- Include time anchor ("Monday morning" not "soon")
- Pair with relevant testimonial when possible

### 7F. About/Bio Template (30% Romei / 50% Toon / 20% Sinek)

```
Short Bio (50 words):
"[Role] che [unique value proposition using core metaphor]. Così [outcome for others], non [alternative bad outcome]."

Example:
"Product Manager che traduce tra business, design e codice. Così i team costruiscono la cosa giusta, non solo qualcosa."

Extended Bio (150 words):
[Paragraph 1]: Start with failure or unconventional path. "Ho fallito come..."
[Paragraph 2]: What that taught you. The "aha" moment. "Dopo [N] anni ho capito..."
[Paragraph 3]: How that serves others now. The "why." "Oggi [what you do and why it matters]."
```

**Rules:**
- Never start with credentials
- Include at least one admission of failure or imperfection
- End with value to others, not self-promotion
- Subvert the "I've been doing X for Y years" format

### 7G. Email Sequence Template (35% Romei / 45% Toon / 20% Sinek)

```
Subject: [Curiosity hook — max 8 words, no clickbait]

[Name],

[Opening: relatable scenario in 1-2 sentences]

[Story/insight: 3-5 sentences with specific detail]

**[Key insight bolded — the one thing they should remember]**

[Practical application: what they can do today]

[Sign-off: warm, brief, human]

[Name]

P.S. [One-liner that adds value or creates curiosity for next email]
```

**Rules:**
- Subject lines: 4-8 words, sentence case, no exclamation marks
- First sentence must be relatable ("If your last sprint planning felt like...")
- One key insight per email, no more
- P.S. always present — highest-read part of email
- "Over to you" or "What do you think?" to invite response

### 7H. Social Media Template (30% Romei / 50% Toon / 20% Sinek)

```
[Hook line — max 12 words, stops the scroll]

[2-3 sentences of specific, human story]

[One insight or contrarian take. Bold if platform allows.]

[Conversation starter question]

Example (IT):
"7 stakeholder. Tutti urgenti. Tutti 'strategici'."

La settimana scorsa, un PM mi ha detto: "Ho 7 feature request e nessuno vuole decidere cosa tagliare."

**Il problema non è avere troppe priorità. È non avere nessuna.**

Cosa tagliereste per primo?
```

**Rules:**
- First line must work as a standalone hook
- Specific numbers and details always
- End with a question to invite engagement
- No hashtag walls — 2-3 max if any
- Never sound like a motivational poster

## 8. ITALIAN / ENGLISH CONSISTENCY GUIDELINES

Based on the language variants audit, Italian is the primary voice. English must preserve the same emotional register, not just translate words.

### 8A. Core Principle: Adapt, Don't Translate

| Rule | Explanation | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Italian is the source of truth** | Always write in Italian first, then adapt to English | Never write English first and translate to Italian |
| **Preserve emotional weight** | If the Italian word carries weight, find the English equivalent weight — not the dictionary equivalent | "errori costosi" → "costly mistakes" (not "expensive mistakes") |
| **Keep inclusive CTA forms** | Italian uses "noi" (we) forms in CTAs. English must replicate this. | "Prenotiamo" → "Let's book" (not "Book") |
| **Adapt idioms, don't translate them** | Italian idioms need English idioms, not literal translations | "tirare a indovinare" → "throwing darts in the dark" (not "guessing") |
| **Preserve all content** | Never cut content from one language version | "Senza traduttori in mezzo" MUST appear in English as "Without translators in between" |

### 8B. Known Inconsistencies to Avoid

These are documented issues from the selfrules.org audit. Check every English version against this list:

| Italian (Correct) | English (Wrong) | English (Correct) | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Lavoriamo insieme: 3 modi per smettere di girare in circolo" | "3 ways to stop going in circles" | "Work together: 3 ways to stop going in circles" | 🔴 Critical |
| "Senza traduttori in mezzo." | *(missing)* | "Without translators in between." | 🔴 Critical |
| "Prenotiamo 15 minuti" | "Book 15 minutes" | "Let's book 15 minutes" | 🔴 Critical |
| "errori costosi" | "expensive mistakes" | "costly mistakes" | ⚠️ Moderate |
| "faccio l'interprete" | "I translate" | "I play translator" | ⚠️ Moderate |
| "rimandi da stamattina" | "postponing since this morning" | "putting off since this morning" | ⚠️ Moderate |
| "che non si capiscono" | "not talking" | "not understanding each other" | ⚠️ Moderate |
| "tirare a indovinare" | "guessing" | "throwing darts in the dark" | ⚠️ Moderate |
| "lavoriamo insieme" | "Work with me" | "Let's work together" | ⚠️ Moderate |

### 8C. Bilingual Writing Checklist

Before publishing any bilingual content:

- [ ] Italian version written first
- [ ] English version adapted (not machine-translated)
- [ ] All CTAs use inclusive "let's" / "noi" forms in both languages
- [ ] No content missing from either version (check line by line)
- [ ] Collaborative framing preserved in both ("lavoriamo insieme" ↔ "let's work together")
- [ ] Idiomatic expressions adapted to equivalent English idioms (not literal translations)
- [ ] Emotional weight of key words matches (see table 8B)
- [ ] Core metaphor (translator/interpreter) preserved in both versions
- [ ] Ironic air quotes preserved (e.g., 'strategici' ↔ 'strategic')
- [ ] Time anchors identical ("Lunedì" = "Monday", "entro Q2" = "by Q2")

### 8D. Language-Specific Voice Profile

| Metric | Italian Target | English Target | Notes |
|--------|---------------|----------------|-------|
| Romei weight | 55% | 55% | Maintain parity |
| Toon weight | 30% | 25-30% | English may drop ~5% due to idiom translation loss — compensate with extra human details |
| Sinek weight | 15% | 15-20% | English can lean slightly more explicit on purpose to compensate for Toon loss |
| Emotional punch | Baseline (100%) | 85-100% of Italian | Target: lose no more than 15% of emotional register |
| Formality level | Informal-professional | Informal-professional | Both versions should sound like a smart friend, not a consultant |

## 9. IMPLEMENTATION GUIDE

### 9A. How to Use This Framework

1. **Identify your content type** → Look up calibration ratios in Section 2
2. **Use the template** → Copy the relevant template from Section 7
3. **Write the Italian version first** → This is the source of truth
4. **Run the quality checklists** → Section 3, prioritized by content type
5. **Check readability** → Section 6, paste into Hemingway App
6. **Adapt to English** → Follow Section 8 guidelines
7. **Run bilingual checklist** → Section 8C before publishing

### 9B. Quick Decision Matrix

When you're stuck, ask:

| Question | If YES | If NO |
|----------|--------|-------|
| Can I cut this word? | Cut it. (Romei) | Keep it. |
| Would a friend say this? | Good. (Toon) | Rewrite. |
| Does the reader know WHY? | Good. (Sinek) | Add the "because." |
| Is this a scene or an abstraction? | Good. | Make it a scene. |
| Could this appear on any consulting website? | Rewrite. | Good — it's yours. |

| When | What | Who |

| Every 6 months | Review signature phrases — retire overused ones, add new ones | Content owner + audience feedback |

## APPENDIX: VOICE SELF-ASSESSMENT SCORECARD

Use this scorecard to rate any piece of content against the Pragmatic Provocateur voice. Score each item 1-5.

| # | Criterion | 1 (Poor) | 3 (Acceptable) | 5 (Excellent) | Score |
|---|-----------|----------|-----------------|----------------|-------|
| 1 | **Clarity** | Requires re-reading, jargon present | Mostly clear, minor complexity | Instant understanding, zero jargon | /5 |
| 2 | **Brevity** | Could cut 40%+ | Could cut 10-20% | Every word essential | /5 |
| 3 | **Relatability** | Sounds like a press release | Has 1-2 human moments | Reader thinks "that's me!" throughout | /5 |
| 4 | **Purpose** | No "why" connected | Implied purpose | Explicit "why" with before/after | /5 |
| 5 | **Specificity** | Generic claims, no numbers | Some specifics | Concrete names, numbers, timelines | /5 |
| 6 | **Voice Uniqueness** | Could appear on any website | Some brand personality | Unmistakably selfrules.org | /5 |
| 7 | **Emotional Punch** | Flat, informational | Mild engagement | Stops the scroll, creates reaction | /5 |
| 8 | **Actionability** | No clear next step | Vague suggestion | Specific action with time anchor | /5 |

**Scoring:**
- 32-40: Ready to publish
- 24-31: Needs one more edit pass
- 16-23: Significant rewrite needed
- Below 16: Start over with the template
